# Sutra Repository

The Sutra Repository is the monastery’s library, the building where Buddhist scriptures and important Buddhist documents are kept. It represents the preservation and transmission of Buddhist classics.

Buddhism calls the Buddha, Dharma, and Saṅgha the “Three Jewels.” Among them, the “Dharma” mainly refers to the Buddha’s teachings, recorded in the scriptures. Therefore, the Sutra Repository is not an ordinary book tower, but a place that preserves the Buddhadharma and transmits wisdom.

In traditional monastery layouts, the Sutra Repository is often built behind the Mahavira Hall, relatively toward the rear and in a quieter location. This arrangement carries a meaning: after entering the monastery, one first pays respect to the Buddhas and bodhisattvas, and then proceeds further to study the scriptures and understand the Buddhadharma. In other words, the Sutra Repository represents the movement from faith toward learning, and from worship toward understanding.

In ancient times, many monastery Sutra Repositories preserved the Buddhist canon, works by patriarchs, woodblocks for printing scriptures, and monastery documents. Some monasteries also held sutra lectures, copying, collation, and printing here. They often also treasured Dharma implements, transmitted paintings and calligraphy, and other core cultural relics of the monastery. It was not only a place for storing books, but also an important space for the transmission of Buddhist culture and thought.

Simply put, the Sutra Repository is the “treasury of wisdom” within the monastery.
